🌱 The Bioscience Problem

In Malaysian beef production, feed accounts for over 70% of production expenses. For native and crossbred Kedah-Kelantan (KK) cattle, growth does not follow a linear path. Instead, it follows a sigmoidal pattern modeled using a logistic growth curve.

How Derivatives Help: The total weight $f(t)$ only tells you how heavy the animal is. To manage resources, we need the Absolute Growth Rate (AGR), which is the first derivative:

$$\text{AGR} = f'(t) \approx \text{Daily Weight Gain}$$

By pinpointing the exact month where the derivative $f'(t)$ reaches its maximum (the inflection point), animal scientists can determine the animal's peak nutrient conversion efficiency and plan the optimal finish-marketing schedule.

📐 Mathematical Model

Cattle Live Weight Function $f(t)$:
$$f(t) = \frac{W_m}{1 + A \cdot e^{-k(t - t_0)}}$$
The First Derivative $f'(t)$ (AGR):
$$f'(t) = \frac{W_m \cdot A \cdot k \cdot e^{-k(t - t_0)}}{(1 + A \cdot e^{-k(t - t_0)})^2}$$

Variables & Parameters:

  • $t$: Animal age in months.
  • $W_m$: Asymptotic maximum mature weight ($350\text{ kg}$).
  • $k$: Growth rate coefficient ($0.15$ to $0.22$).
  • $t_0$: Ideal inflection point (centered around $12\text{ months}$).
  • $A$: Integration/scaling constant relative to initial birth weight.
